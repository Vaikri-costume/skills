# Lenses: genre, field and the three blind readers

## Genre lenses (added to the Argument and Evidence briefs)

Choose by what the draft is. If none fits, use none.
- **Argument or theoretical paper:** the claim is contestable and stated early; every section advances it; the strongest objection is met.
- **Dissertation chapter:** the chapter's job within the whole is stated; it ends by saying what the next chapter inherits; sources are positioned, not listed.
- **Research proposal:** the question, the gap, the method and the contribution are each findable; the method can answer the question as posed.

A genre lens adds questions to look for; it never adds a rule the draft must meet.

## Field lens

Only from the writer's field bank, read at run time (`profile.md`). Paste the relevant page into the Argument, Evidence and Voice briefs as "what this field expects, as agreed with the writer". Never import a field's norms from memory; where no page exists, no field lens.

## Three blind lenses (the Lens pass)

Three read-only agents each read only the draft (no other agent output, no findings) with one lens, and return questions, not verdicts:
- **Audience:** a smart reader outside the topic. Where do you lose the thread, and what do you need to be told?
- **Rigour:** a sceptical specialist. Which claims would you want evidence for, and where does the evidence not reach the claim?
- **Premise:** someone who rejects the starting assumption. Which unstated premise does the argument depend on?

Each question is anchored to one quoted line from the draft (`Current text:`), and says what in the text prompted it. The report shows the three lenses side by side; where they disagree they are shown, never averaged or reconciled. A question is withdrawn, and the withdrawal shown, when the draft elsewhere answers it; say where.

Output format for each lens agent:
```
## Finding [n]
Location: paragraph [n] - "[opening words]"
Current text: "[exact quote]"
Lens: audience / rigour / premise
Question: [one question to the writer]
Prompted by: [what in the text led to it]
```
or `## No findings` with one sentence.

## Limits

- The field lens exists only where the writer's field bank has a page for the field; with none, say so and apply no field lens.
