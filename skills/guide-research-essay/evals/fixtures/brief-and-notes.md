# Invented brief and notes (test fixtures)

## Brief
Question: Did public clocks change who controlled working time in a nineteenth-century town?
Limit: 1500 words.
Required sections, in this order: introduction; evidence; counter-argument; conclusion.

## Notes (all from the Marrowby reading)
- N1 (p. 101): the mill bell rang at six, noon and seven.
- N2 (p. 101): in 1846 three weavers were fined for "disregarding the bell".
- N3 (p. 102): the clock (1853) cost 212 pounds, paid for by subscription from shopkeepers.
- N4 (p. 102): the author concedes the council minutes never state the clock's purpose.
- N5 (p. 103): the bell rang until 1874; the author suggests, without evidence beyond one diary, that some households preferred it.
- N6 (p. 103): after 1860 the railway timetable required the clock to be set to a single standard.
