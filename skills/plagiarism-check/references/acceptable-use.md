# What counts as plagiarism here, the status ladder and the limits

One standard, used by the judgement brief, the challenger and the presenter. It describes risk for the writer to review; it does not rule on misconduct.

## Acceptable use (not flagged)

- Common knowledge: facts a general reader of the field would know without a source.
- Standard formulations: definitions, method names, legal or procedural phrases that have one usual wording.
- Convergent phrasing: two writers expressing a simple idea in similar words, with no distinctive turn of phrase or order shared.
- Field vocabulary: terms the source itself treats as established (no definition, no citation for the term).
- A marked quotation with a citation, even where the quotation marks were lost only in formatting (poor academic practice: restore the marks).

## Not acceptable (flag, with the status below)

- Distinctive language: an unusual phrase, image or sentence taken without quotation marks, even when cited.
- Distinctive structure: the source's order of argument, steps or examples followed under new wording.
- Distinctive examples: a source's own illustration or case used as if the writer found it.
- Frameworks: a source's central analytical lens or terms used without crediting it.

## Status ladder (one per finding, strongest first)

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| verbatim | six or more identical consecutive words outside quotation marks, or a longer run with only trivial changes |
| near-verbatim | the source's sentence with words swapped or reordered: the draft sentence can be mapped onto the source sentence word for word, same skeleton, same clause count |
| structurally derived | the sentences are rebuilt (they cannot be mapped word for word) but the passage follows the source's sequence of ideas, examples or ranking; name the shared elements in the source's order |
| possibly derived | a resemblance that may be convergent; the check cannot tell, so the writer decides |

A passage that meets none of these is not a finding. Status is about the text; weight (poor academic practice, minor, major) is about how much of the draft and how central the idea is.

## Limits (state them in every report)

- The check compares the draft only with the sources it was given. It cannot find unknown sources, other writers' work or essay-bank text; the institution's similarity report does that. No draft text is sent anywhere.
- Clean means nothing found through the search used. It does not mean the draft is original.
- Flags are for the writer's review, not findings of misconduct; intent is judged by a marker on the balance of probabilities.
- The thresholds are this skill's heuristics (the verbatim rung above and the weak four-to-five-word tier in `overlap_check.py`); institutions publish none.
