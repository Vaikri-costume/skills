# SOAS categories and limits (worked UK higher-education example)

The categories below follow SOAS University of London's public misconduct policy as a worked UK higher-education example. Adapt the category names and thresholds to your own institution's policy.

Source: SOAS Academic Misconduct Procedure, REG-183-10 v10 (approved 27 Aug 2025, effective 1 Sep 2026), §2.2 to 2.9. Re-check the current version before relying on section numbers.

## Categories used in the report

| Report label | SOAS wording | Section |
|---|---|---|
| verbatim without reference | verbatim copying of another's work without appropriate referencing | §2.3(a) |
| close paraphrase | close paraphrasing by changing a few words or altering the order of presentation, without appropriate referencing | §2.3(b) |
| unacknowledged quotation or paraphrase | unacknowledged quotation or paraphrases from another's work or from the student's own work | §2.3(c) |
| self-plagiarism | unacknowledged re-use of one's own earlier work; large reuse can count even if referenced | §2.3(d), §2.6 |
| citation practice | "incorrect, inadequate or confused citation", not intended to gain advantage | §2.5 (poor academic practice) |

Also defined by SOAS but outside this skill: collusion, contract cheating, unauthorised generative AI (§2.3(e) to (g); see ai-detection-check for the authenticity view). A series of short unidentified quotations from several sources is plagiarism as much as one long one (§2.2).

## Weight

- poor-academic-practice: inexperience-type citation errors; handled through normal marking.
- minor: a small amount of paraphrase or quotation without adequate attribution (§2.4(a)); becomes major if the material carries critical ideas key to the assignment.
- major: extensive paraphrasing or quoting without citation; lifting directly without reference (§2.4(b)).

## What this skill cannot do (state this in every report)

- It compares the draft only with the sources it was given. It cannot find unknown sources, other students' work, or essay-bank text; SOAS markers use the Turnitin similarity report for that.
- It reports flags, not findings of misconduct. Intent is judged by the marker, and the standard is the balance of probabilities (§3.1).
- Numeric overlap thresholds in this skill (6 words verbatim, 4 to 5 weak) are heuristics. SOAS and QAA publish none.
