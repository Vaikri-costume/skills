# Misconduct categories and weights

Vocabulary common to UK university academic-misconduct procedures. The writer's institution may word and number these differently: when the profile key `institution_rules` names a file, use the labels and section numbers it holds as the report headings; otherwise use the labels below. The standard for what counts, the status ladder and the limits are in `acceptable-use.md`.

## Categories used in the report

| Report label | Typical wording |
|---|---|
| verbatim without reference | verbatim copying of another's work without appropriate referencing |
| close paraphrase | close paraphrasing by changing a few words or altering the order of presentation, without appropriate referencing |
| unacknowledged quotation or paraphrase | unacknowledged quotation or paraphrase from another's work or from the writer's own earlier work |
| self-plagiarism | unacknowledged re-use of one's own earlier work; large reuse can count even if referenced |
| citation practice | incorrect, inadequate or confused citation, not intended to gain advantage (poor academic practice) |

Also defined in most procedures but outside this skill: collusion, contract cheating and unauthorised generative AI (see ai-detection-check for the authenticity view). A series of short unidentified quotations from several sources is plagiarism as much as one long one.

## Weight

- poor-academic-practice: inexperience-type citation errors; handled through normal marking.
- minor: a small amount of paraphrase or quotation without adequate attribution; becomes major if the material carries ideas key to the assignment.
- major: extensive paraphrasing or quoting without citation; lifting directly without reference.
