# Misconduct categories and limits

Vocabulary common to UK university academic-misconduct procedures. Your own institution may word and number these differently: adapt the category names and thresholds to its policy.

## Categories used in the report

| Report label | Typical wording |
|---|---|
| verbatim without reference | verbatim copying of another's work without appropriate referencing |
| close paraphrase | close paraphrasing by changing a few words or altering the order of presentation, without appropriate referencing |
| unacknowledged quotation or paraphrase | unacknowledged quotation or paraphrases from another's work or from the student's own work |
| self-plagiarism | unacknowledged re-use of one's own earlier work; large reuse can count even if referenced |
| citation practice | incorrect, inadequate or confused citation, not intended to gain advantage (poor academic practice) |

Also defined in most procedures but outside this skill: collusion, contract cheating, unauthorised generative AI (see ai-detection-check for the authenticity view). A series of short unidentified quotations from several sources is plagiarism as much as one long one.

## Weight

- poor-academic-practice: inexperience-type citation errors; handled through normal marking.
- minor: a small amount of paraphrase or quotation without adequate attribution; becomes major if the material carries critical ideas key to the assignment.
- major: extensive paraphrasing or quoting without citation; lifting directly without reference.

## What this skill cannot do (state this in every report)

- It compares the draft only with the sources it was given. It cannot find unknown sources, other students' work, or essay-bank text; markers use the Turnitin similarity report for that.
- It reports flags, not findings of misconduct. Intent is judged by the marker, and the standard is the balance of probabilities.
- Numeric overlap thresholds in this skill (6 words verbatim, 4 to 5 weak) are heuristics. Institutions and QAA publish none.
